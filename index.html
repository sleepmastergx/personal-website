Latent world modeling ICLR 2027 · Under review

La-JEPA
Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling

Compact robot policies trained in a pretrained JEPA feature space, learning actions and future features jointly while predicting only actions at deployment.

Method & findings

Approach. A policy transformer trained from scratch uses a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder. Joint feature and action prediction allows the policy architecture to be designed for control.

Reported results. La-JEPA L reaches 82.3% on LIBERO-Plus. Under the clean-trained RoboTwin 2.0 protocol, La-JEPA B reports 61.8% mean success across clean and randomized evaluations, in comparisons without embodied pretraining.

Scope. Physical evaluations cover four tasks on one setup. The results do not establish broad real-world parity with larger pretrained policies.

Efficient robot policies ICLR 2027 · Under review

Faster-WAM
Do World Action Models Need Deep Action Modules?

A world-model-centric robot policy that connects a single-layer action expert to a pretrained video backbone, reducing action-specific computation while retaining control performance.

Method & findings

Approach. Dock of Transformers and Lite KV-Fusion give a shallow action expert access to video-model representations. World-model-only conditioning and retracted 1D-RoPE align the action and video branches.

Reported results. 98.3% success on LIBERO and 78.3% on LIBERO-Plus, with a 3.7–3.8× speedup over Fast-WAM in matched comparisons. Physical tests report 81.3% mean success, compared with 80.0% for Fast-WAM.

Scope. Evaluations cover two video backbones and four physical tasks on one platform. A single action layer is not established as universally optimal.

Memory & evaluation CoRL 2026 Memory Workshop · Submitted

Decodable Cues, Unreliable Execution
Diagnosing TTT Memory in World–Action Models

A diagnostic study separating what robot memory stores, whether it changes behavior, and whether it improves task execution.

Method & findings

Approach. Fast-weight memory is evaluated with task-cue decoders and controlled right-, wrong-, and empty-memory interventions across three manipulation tasks.

Reported findings. On MoveCube, cues are decoded correctly in 60 of 60 inspected cases, yet task success is 11.7%. On PickHighlight, success is 12.1%, compared with 0% with empty memory. Stored information and reliable execution need separate evaluation.

Scope. An exploratory case study using inspected validation sets and one training seed per recipe. No memory-latency benchmark is reported.

Agentic aerial robotics Public preprint · 2025

Agentic Aerial Cinematography
From Dialogue Cues to Cinematic Trajectories

An agentic framework that translates natural-language filming requests into scene-grounded viewpoints and executable drone trajectories, combining visual feedback with optimization and motion planning.

Paper ↗ (opens in a new tab) Project website ↗ (opens in a new tab)

Equal-contributing author · Listed as George Z. Xue

Method & evaluation

Approach. Vision-language shot retrieval and ordering, preference-based Bayesian camera-pose refinement, and collision-aware, dynamics-constrained trajectory planning connect filming intent to onboard control.

Evaluation. Six simulated tours, a 41-participant user study, and hardware-in-the-loop flight tests. Prompt-alignment ratings range from 3.8 to 4.2 out of 5.

Scope. The framework assumes a static, mapped scene. Hardware-in-the-loop tests use a physical quadrotor, while cinematic views remain simulated; the study does not establish a comparative aesthetic advantage over human filming.
